NORD RESEARCH BRIEF — EMAIL VISUAL STYLE GUIDE
Version 1.0, September 2026

PURPOSE

This is the house style for compact research-digest emails: calm, technical,
selective, and easy to scan before the reader is fully awake. It should feel
like a carefully edited lab brief, not a newsletter, dashboard, marketing
email, or social feed. The visual language is Nord-inspired: cool neutrals,
deep blue-black text, restrained Frost-blue emphasis, and sparse Aurora colors
used only to communicate priority.

The email should look polished on a Mac and completely natural on an iPhone.
It uses whitespace and typography to create hierarchy; decoration stays quiet.
There are no hero images, gradients, drop shadows, badges everywhere, novelty
fonts, or ornamental icons. The papers are the visual content.


DESIGN PRINCIPLES

1. Editorial, not promotional. Lead with the date, queue size, and the papers.
   Never add slogans, engagement prompts, banners, or oversized branding.

2. High information density without visual pressure. Every card contains real
   metadata, one compact relevance rationale, a 3–4 sentence summary, and three
   links, but generous line height and consistent spacing keep it readable.

3. Priority must be visible without relying on color alone. Each section has a
   written label, a count, and a colored left rail. “Less urgent” is clearly
   separated from “Read first”; it never masquerades as an equally strong pick.

4. Nord is the source palette, not a theme gimmick. Cool grays and blue-black
   ink dominate. Frost blue is the normal accent. Aurora purple and orange are
   reserved for revisions and lower urgency. Saturated color never fills a
   large area.

5. Email constraints are part of the aesthetic. The design should remain
   elegant with table layout, inline CSS, system fonts, and no external assets.


VISUAL PERSONALITY

Keywords: quiet, rigorous, wintry, lucid, bookish, modern, trustworthy,
technical, restrained, slightly academic.

Avoid: glossy, playful, gamified, corporate, newspaper-like, cyberpunk,
terminal-themed, maximalist, card-grid dashboard, or AI-generated-looking.

The target impression is “a very good research assistant prepared this before
breakfast.”

Rules for color

  - Use blue as the default interactive and high-priority accent.
  - Use purple only for materially revised papers.
  - Use orange only for “Less urgent” items and actionable source warnings.
  - Never color a full paper card. Keep the card white and apply color through
    a 5 px left rail, a small uppercase label, and a pale rationale inset.
  - Never use red or green as general decoration.
  - Do not introduce a second blue, orange, or purple without replacing the
    corresponding token everywhere.


PAGE AND CONTAINER GEOMETRY

The message is one centered column on a cool-gray canvas.

  Maximum shell width            640 px
  Shell width                    100% up to the maximum
  Desktop outer padding          28 px vertical, 14 px horizontal
  Shell background               white
  Shell border                   1 px solid #DFE3E8
  Shell corner radius            14 px
  Main desktop content padding   30 px top, 31 px sides, 13 px bottom
  Footer desktop padding         17 px top, 31 px sides, 28 px bottom

Do not use a two-column layout. Do not add a persistent sidebar, table of
contents, or navigation bar. Long messages remain easy to scan because every
paper repeats the same card grammar.


TYPOGRAPHY

Font stack

  Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif

This deliberately uses system-safe sans serif rather than a webfont. The email
must render consistently in Gmail, Apple Mail, and browser previews without a
font download. Do not use a monospace font for metadata; that would make the
message feel like a log rather than an editorial brief.

Type hierarchy

  Masthead eyebrow               12 px / 18 px, weight 700,
                                 letter-spacing 0.09 em, uppercase, blue

  Main heading                   29 px / 36 px, weight 700, primary ink

  Header count line              14 px / 21 px, regular, muted ink

  Section heading                17 px / 23 px, weight 700, primary ink

  Section subtitle               13 px / 19 px, regular, muted ink

  Section count                  12 px / 18 px, weight 700, section accent

  Card priority label            11 px / 15 px, weight 700,
                                 letter-spacing 0.08 em, uppercase

  Paper title                    20 px / 27 px, weight 700, primary ink

  Authors                        14 px / 21 px, regular, secondary ink

  Version/category/date          12 px / 18 px, regular, muted ink

  Why-it-matches line            14 px / 21 px, body ink;
                                 the label itself is weight 700, primary ink

  Technical summary              15 px / 24 px, regular, body ink

  Link buttons                   13 px / 18 px, weight 700

  Footer heading                 13 px / 20 px, weight 700, primary ink
  Footer body                    13 px / 20 px, regular, muted ink
  Footer acknowledgement        11 px / 17 px, regular, faint ink

Use sentence case for headings except the small masthead and card-priority
labels. Do not italicize abstracts, author names, or metadata. Do not truncate
paper titles; allow natural wrapping with overflow-wrap enabled.


MESSAGE HIERARCHY

The message always follows this order:

  Hidden inbox preheader
  -> masthead eyebrow with product name and date
  -> “Today’s papers” title
  -> screened and selected counts
  -> optional monthly-focus panel
  -> optional source/catch-up notice
  -> optional Important revisions section
  -> Read first section
  -> optional Less urgent section
  -> compact feedback footer
  -> arXiv acknowledgement

The first visible screen should answer three questions immediately: what this
is, how many papers survived screening, and what the active research focus is.


MASTHEAD AND CONTEXT PANELS

The masthead is intentionally small. “ARXIV BRIEF · DATE” is a blue uppercase
eyebrow, followed by the much larger “Today’s papers.” The screened/selected
line sits beneath in muted gray. There is no logo.

Monthly focus panel

  Background                     #F1F4F8
  Radius                         9 px
  Margin                         17 px top, 4 px bottom
  Padding                        12 px vertical, 14 px horizontal
  Copy                           13 px / 20 px

It begins with a bold “This month:” label and one plain-language sentence. It
should read as context, not an alert.

Source/catch-up notice

  Background                     #FFF5E7
  Radius                         9 px
  Margin                         12 px top, 4 px bottom
  Padding                        12 px vertical, 14 px horizontal
  Copy                           13 px / 20 px, dark amber

Use this only when source completeness, cached personalization, or another
run-level condition materially affects interpretation. Routine success does
not receive a green banner.


SECTION HEADERS

Each section header has a title and one-line explanation on the left, with the
paper count aligned right in the section accent. The block has 25 px of top
margin and 11 px of bottom margin. Keep the title descriptive and calm:

  Important revisions
  “Previously seen papers with a material update worth reopening.”

  Read first
  “The strongest matches for your active research interests.”

  Less urgent
  “Relevant and worth saving, but lower priority for today.”

Never rename “Less urgent” to “Recommended,” because the explicit difference
in urgency is part of the product’s honesty.


PAPER CARD ANATOMY

Every paper uses one full-width white card.

  Card border                    1 px solid #DFE3E8
  Card radius                    12 px
  Gap after card                 14 px
  Priority rail                 5 px wide, section accent
  Desktop inner padding         20 px top, 22 px sides, 19 px bottom

The internal order is fixed:

1. Index and priority label. Example: “1 · READ FIRST.” This is small,
   uppercase, tracked text in the section accent.

2. Full paper title. It is the strongest element inside the card.

3. Authors. Show names as normal prose; do not turn every author into a chip.

4. Metadata. Use “v1 · new · cond-mat.stat-mech · Wed, Sep 2, 2026.” Middle
   dots provide quiet separation without adding boxes or columns.

5. Why-it-matches inset. Use the section’s pale tint with an 8 px radius,
   12 px vertical and 14 px horizontal padding, and 14 px bottom margin. The
   rationale is exactly one concise sentence naming concrete methods, results,
   or ideas. It is not a second summary.

6. Technical summary. Use exactly 3–4 short sentences covering the problem,
   method, principal result, and limitation or reason to care. Do not add a
   heading such as “Summary”; the typography already establishes its role.

7. Link row. Place Abstract, HTML, and PDF in that order.

Do not display raw ranking scores, confidence values, keyword clouds, model
names, or internal cluster IDs. Those are diagnostics, not reading decisions.


BUTTONS AND LINKS

Primary button: Abstract

  Background                     #172033
  Text                           #FFFFFF
  Padding                        10 px vertical, 13 px horizontal
  Radius                         7 px
  Border                         none

Secondary buttons: HTML and PDF

  Background                     #FFFFFF
  Text                           #263247
  Border                         1 px solid #C9D0DA
  Padding                        10 px vertical, 13 px horizontal
  Radius                         7 px

Use 8 px horizontal gaps. Buttons are compact text controls, not wide calls to
action. Do not add icons; the three words are clearer and survive every client.
All destinations must be absolute HTTPS links.


SPACING RHYTHM

The implicit base unit is 4 px. Preferred increments are 4, 8, 12, 16, 20,
24, 28, and 32 px; the implementation uses nearby optical adjustments such as
7, 9, 11, 13, 14, 17, 19, 22, 25, and 31 px where typography needs them.

Within a card, related metadata is separated by 7–9 px, major content blocks
by 14–16 px, and cards by 14 px. Between sections, use about 25 px. Resist the
urge to compress the summary just because a digest is long; reduce the number
of papers instead.


MOBILE BEHAVIOR

The breakpoint is 680 px. Below it:

  Shell width                    100%
  Outer padding                  14 px vertical, 10 px horizontal
  Main/footer side padding       17 px
  Card padding                   17 px vertical, 16 px horizontal
  Paper title                    18 px / 25 px

At a 390 px viewport, the shell should be 370 px wide with 10 px margins and
zero horizontal overflow. Titles, authors, metadata, rationales, and summaries
wrap naturally. The link row should remain compact enough to fit; if a future
client adds longer action names, stack the buttons rather than shrinking text.

Do not hide metadata or summaries on mobile. Mobile receives the same editorial
content, with tighter padding and a slightly smaller paper title.


EMAIL-CLIENT IMPLEMENTATION RULES

  - Use presentation tables for macro layout and cards.
  - Put critical styles inline; a small head style block may contain only the
    responsive overrides and Apple data-detector correction.
  - Include width=device-width and declare a light color scheme.
  - Use no JavaScript, forms, iframes, objects, embeds, inputs, or images.
  - Use no CSS grid or flexbox.
  - Use no external fonts, stylesheets, trackers, or remote decorative assets.
  - Keep the HTML below 90 KiB so Gmail does not clip it.
  - Include a complete plain-text alternative with the same section order and
    every Abstract, HTML, and PDF URL.
  - Escape all paper titles, authors, abstracts, and generated rationale text.
  - Use color-independent text labels for every priority state.


ACCESSIBILITY

Use semantic h1, h2, and h3 headings even inside presentation tables. Body copy
should stay at 15 px / 24 px, with high-contrast blue-black text on white. Link
labels must describe destinations directly. Priority is conveyed by section
name, card label, and position as well as color. Never place essential meaning
in a tooltip, hover effect, image, or colored dot.

The three action buttons should remain comfortably tappable on a phone. Avoid
gray text lighter than #8992A3, and reserve that faint tone for nonessential
acknowledgement copy.


COPY AND EDITORIAL TONE

Visual consistency depends on verbal consistency. Use short, literal labels
and technically specific prose.

Preferred labels:

  Today’s papers
  This month:
  Important revisions
  Read first
  Less urgent
  Why it matches:
  Abstract / HTML / PDF
  Tune tomorrow’s ranking

The relevance sentence names methods and results: “Uses zero-temperature
replicas to derive large-deviation rate functions…” It never says only “This
matches your interests.” Summaries avoid hype and distinguish a new result from
an expository chapter, preliminary method, or adjacent application.

No emojis, exclamation marks, fake urgency, or engagement language. Counts are
factual. Empty sections disappear. Weak papers do not fill a quota.


FOOTER

Separate the footer from the paper queue with a 1 px #E5E8ED rule. The footer
contains one compact heading, one explanation of how feedback affects future
ranking, and a faint arXiv acknowledgement. It should feel like the endnote of
a memo, not a second navigation area.

Do not add social links, legal boilerplate, promotional copy, or a large logo.


CONSISTENCY CHECKLIST FOR A NEW EMAIL CLIENT

  [ ] Cool-gray canvas, white 640 px shell, thin cool-gray border.
  [ ] Arial/Helvetica system typography with the hierarchy above.
  [ ] Small blue masthead, strong left-aligned title, visible queue counts.
  [ ] Optional neutral monthly-focus panel and amber run notice.
  [ ] One vertical reading flow; no dashboard grid.
  [ ] White paper cards with a 5 px priority rail.
  [ ] Read-first blue, revision purple, less-urgent orange.
  [ ] One-sentence why-it-matches inset plus a 3–4 sentence summary.
  [ ] Dark Abstract button; outlined HTML and PDF buttons.
  [ ] No scores, chips, thumbnails, gradients, shadows, or decorative icons.
  [ ] 390 px mobile layout has no horizontal overflow.
  [ ] HTML remains below 90 KiB and includes a full plain-text alternative.
  [ ] The result feels like an edited research memo rather than a newsletter.


ONE-SENTENCE ART DIRECTION

A quiet Nord-inspired research memo: deep Polar-Night typography on white and
Snow-Storm surfaces, Frost-blue priority cues, restrained Aurora exceptions,
and enough whitespace that dense technical summaries remain calm on a phone.
